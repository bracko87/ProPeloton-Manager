# Race Engine V3 — Step A56: retrospective collision anticipation and ordinary braking lead

**28 October 2026 — technical evidence PASS, no historically validated repaired trajectory, sporting NO-GO.**

## Question

Step55 showed that **zero of 70** A53 single-component substitutions that were swept-safe as straight-line paths were admissible under the entire field's provisional ordinary acceleration/braking limits. One rider could need over **28m/s²** deceleration to reach a position generated by the late contact solver. Correcting only the terminal speed would not restore the missing forward displacement.

A56 tests a narrower, safer diagnostic hypothesis: **could the model have known earlier that contact was approaching, and how early would ordinary braking need to begin?** The answer requires a distinction between (a) an optimistic timing calculation, (b) a prior warning visible in the existing simulation record, and (c) a new collision-free full-pack trajectory. This step implements and measures **(a) and (b) only** on historical cases, while proving (c) is possible solely in a controlled two-cyclist synthetic example.

### Stopping-distance math and explicitly limited lead estimate

For starting speed `v ≥ 0`, braking magnitude `b = 0.85m/s²`, and horizon `h`:

- If `h ≤ v/b`, the *minimum* continuous ordinary-braking forward distance is `v·h - b·h²/2`.
- If `h > v/b`, the cyclist stops after `v/b` and the total forward distance is `v²/(2b)`.

A rider already short of the lawful current-substep minimum is not salvageable by a new terminal speed at the end of that substep. A56 estimates additional lead `L` from the extra distance saved by starting an otherwise maximal braking profile **before** the current boundary, compared with starting only at that boundary. It finds the smallest `L` satisfying an idealized speed-preserving, no-terrain-change calculation. This is **not a sufficient condition** for avoidance: it assumes a favorable earlier trajectory, ignores other riders' responses, and may not be realizable from the actual recorded earlier initial state. The output is clearly tagged `optimisticLeadSeconds` and is never used as a new movement command.

### What is a recorded warning?

The analysis reads actual A44 diagnostic substep proposals during the **preceding 10 seconds**, not future values in a live process. A warning is recorded if either:

1. The submitted provisional motion already contains the rider in an observed continuous 2D **swept contact pair**, or
2. A **following rider** and rider ahead start inside the same lateral occupancy corridor and the trailing rider's gap is no larger than `1.8m + closingSpeed × physicalStep + closingSpeed²/(2×0.85)`. This is a provisional headway alarm with one physical-step reaction delay and constant leader speed; it is **not a proof** that the follower can actually avoid contact under real pack responses.

A warning only at the already-infeasible substep is **not** counted as advance warning. The first and last prior alerts are retained with absolute simulated time, type, and the idealized remaining lead.

The original **70** geometric-safe cases, five geometric blockers and zero historical full-field bounded-velocity certificates remain unchanged.

## Positive control: early braking can be physically feasible

In a deliberately simple two-rider straight corridor, a rear cyclist starts at 8m/s and a front cyclist at 6m/s, with a 10m initial headway and a 5-second horizon. If both continue at those speeds, the rear rider's continuous swept path catches the front rider. If the rear rider applies **bounded -0.85m/s² braking over all five seconds**, the rear moves **29.375m** while the front moves **30m**. The exact quadratic rear trajectory and linear front trajectory pass the independent **conservative continuous swept collision certificate**; neither braking nor the final velocity exceeds the provisional ordinary envelope.

The test demonstrates that an early, physically bounded response **can** resolve one synthetic impending collision. It does not show that the same local command is available or harmless in the 32-rider historical cases, and no additional live steering, energy or game-clock operations are authorized.
